# Standardize turnaround messaging

## Goal
Create one clear, credible turnaround story across the site so visitors understand what they receive first, what requires design development, and when to expect each response.

## Recommended messaging hierarchy

1. **Initial response:** A designer acknowledges the brief and confirms fit, missing information, and next steps within **one business day**.
2. **Preliminary concept + investment:** The complimentary first-pass concept direction and an accurate preliminary investment range are delivered within **two business days** after the brief is complete. This is the promise used beside primary CTAs.
3. **Full design development:** Photoreal renderings, floor plans, detailed scope, and a costed BOM are developed in approximately **one to two weeks**, depending on booth size, revisions, engineering, and scope. This is not presented as the initial lead-response promise.
4. **Production timeline:** Keep the separate 6–10 week production language for approved custom builds; it describes fabrication after design approval rather than sales/design response time.

## Changes

- Update the shared CTA reassurance so it no longer promises a 3–5 business-day concept when other site CTAs promise two business days.
- Rewrite the homepage process step to distinguish the two-business-day preliminary response from the one-to-two-week full design-development stage.
- Update the homepage closing CTA and any shared CTA-band copy to use the same two-business-day wording and explain that the first response is preliminary, with the full photoreal/costed BOM following during design development.
- Align the Services hub FAQ and Web Summit Vancouver page FAQ, process, quote section, and final CTA so they do not imply conflicting response times.
- Preserve the existing distinctions between quote response, design development, and fabrication; do not change pricing, lead forms, or business logic.

## Technical details

- Centralize reusable response language in the existing CTA copy constants where practical instead of repeating slightly different claims.
- Keep page-specific wording for Web Summit only where its process genuinely differs, but use the same two-business-day preliminary response and one-to-two-week full design-development distinction.
- After editing, verify the homepage and affected pages in the live preview and search the source for the old 3–5 business-day and two-business-day phrasing to ensure no contradictory public CTA remains.
